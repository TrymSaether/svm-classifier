\section{Conclusion}\label{chap:conclusion}
\vspace{-0.4cm}
This project examined SVMs through their primal and dual formulations, highlighting the kernel trick for non-linear classification. Key properties include convex global optimization, computational advantages of the dual, kernel-based feature mapping, and sparse support-vector solutions. Experiments validated these concepts, with near-identical primal and dual results on linear problems, perfect non-linear performance using RBF, polynomial, and Laplacian kernels, and a 98.25\% accuracy on the Breast Cancer Wisconsin dataset. Despite challenges with large-scale optimization, binary constraints, and parameter tuning, SVMs remain relevant due to their robust theoretical foundations and strong empirical results.
