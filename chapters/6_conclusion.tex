\section{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}
\vspace{-0.4cm}
This project investigated Support Vector Machines theoretically, numerically, and practically. We formulated soft-margin SVMs in both primal form and dual form (enabling kernels), proved solution existence and uniqueness, and verified their equivalence through KKT conditions. Our numerical implementations included gradient descent with smooth approximations, projected gradient descent, adaptive step sizes, and line search techniques. Experiments revealed the critical role of the regularization parameter C, different convergence behaviors across kernels, and superior RBF kernel performance (98.25\% accuracy) on the Breast Cancer Wisconsin dataset. 
In conclusion, SVMs remain a valuable tool, offering theoretical rigor, convex optimization benefits, interpretable decision boundaries, and remarkable efficiency when working with smaller datasets.
